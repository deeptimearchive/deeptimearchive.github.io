In the late 1500s, the sultan of Morocco decided to do something that sounded impossible: send an army across the Sahara, the largest hot desert on Earth, to attack one of the great empires of West Africa. The man he chose to lead it had been born in Spain - and had grown up as a slave.

A boy from Almería

Judar was a native of Cuevas del Almanzora, in the province of Almería in southern Spain. A baptismal record from 1562 - probably the year of his birth - survives there. How he reached North Africa is not certain. He was probably taken as a boy by raiders who struck along the Almanzora River, but no record confirms it.

What is certain is where he ended up: in the service of Ahmad al-Mansur, the Saadian sultan of Morocco. Like many boys who entered the sultan's service, Judar became a eunuch.

The impossible march

In late 1590, al-Mansur announced that he was sending an army of 3,000 to 4,000 troops, with thousands of pack animals, south across the Sahara against the Songhai Empire. Its commander was Judar - now Judar Pasha.

The march crossed the heart of the desert. By one account it took about 135 days before the army stood on the banks of the Niger River.

Guns against cattle

In March 1591 the two armies met at Tondibi, in what is now Mali. The Songhai ruler, Askia Ishaq II, had a surprise: his army drove a herd of cattle straight at the invaders.

The volleys of infantry weapons and cannon frightened the cattle - and they charged back through the Songhai's own lines.

Moroccan gunpowder won the day, and the defeat brought down the Songhai Empire.

Winning wasn't enough

Judar's reward was not what you might expect. His report on local conditions discouraged the sultan, and he was replaced as commander and sent to the lesser post of governor of Gao. His successor, Mahmud ibn Zarqun, chose revenge over peace: leading families of Timbuktu were massacred, and many survivors were loaded with chains and sent to Marrakesh.

Among them was the great scholar Ahmad Baba, deported to Morocco in 1594.

The return

In 1599 Judar Pasha finally went home to Morocco - rich with gold, slaves and exotic goods. By one account, English agents in Marrakesh reported to Queen Elizabeth on the huge fortunes arriving there on his behalf.

A boy from a Spanish village, sold into slavery, ended up toppling an African empire. Some stories really are stranger than fiction.

What we couldn't confirm

Some popular retellings give Judar's birth name as "Diego de Guevara" and say he returned with thirty camel-loads of gold worth £600,000. We could not find independent sources for either detail, so we left them out.
